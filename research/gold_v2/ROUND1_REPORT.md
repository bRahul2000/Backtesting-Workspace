## Why each was rejected

- **C1.** Round $50 levels did **not** produce more reversals than equivalent non-round levels. The point estimate is
  the opposite sign and is negative in every leave-one-year-out subset. The documented mechanism (take-profit
  clustering → reversals; Osler 2003; Aggarwal & Lucey 2007) is not visible in XAUUSDm M15 2023–2025 as a reversal
  effect.
  - **Limitation.** 51% of round events and 45% of placebo events were `ambiguous`: the touch bar itself reached
    the reversal target, so M15 cannot order the touch and the extreme. M15 resolution is coarse relative to a
    level-touch mechanism.
- **C4.** The sign of the US-session return carries no information about the next Asian session: correlation 0.005,
  circular-shift p 0.44. It is not stable by year. The Asia window had positive average drift (mean y ≈ +0.43 ATR),
  which the circular-shift null correctly absorbs. That drift is not a signal, and C5 (unconditional drift) was
  rejected at design for lacking a counterfactual.

## Post-hoc observations (descriptive only — NOT tested, NOT actionable)

- **C1.** Touches of round levels from above were followed by **more continuation** than placebo touches (−9.9 pp).
  This is the direction of Osler's (2005) stop-loss-cascade mechanism. **It was generated by looking at development
  outcomes.** It could only become a hypothesis through a new, separately registered decision, tested on data
  unseen for C1 outcomes (the 2017–2021 replication era has not been used for any C1 outcome). It must not be treated
  as evidence.
- **Cost scale.** The C1 break-even for a 1:1 fade would have been P(rev) ≈ 52.0% (median spread/d = 4.1%). The
  observed round-level P(rev) was 39.9%.
